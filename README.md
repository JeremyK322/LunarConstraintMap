# Lunar Constraint Map

An open, public map of the Moon that makes due regard visible.

The map overlays every known category of lunar constraint on a single spatial reference frame. It makes the spatial dimension of existing legal obligations legible. It does not create new law. It does not enforce anything. It turns on the lights.

The Moon is not crowded yet, but it will be. The most contested assets are not only water ice and metals. They are landing sites, stable orbits, radio-quiet zones, sites of scientific interest, heritage, view, spectrum. These are resources in every meaningful sense, and they are already scarce.

## Layers

Extractable. Water ice in permanently shadowed regions, metals, volatiles, minerals.

Spatial. Landing sites, launch sites, stable orbits, Lagrange points.

Environmental. Permanently shadowed regions, sites of scientific interest, fragile geological features.

Intangible. Heritage sites, cultural values, the view of Earth.

Electromagnetic. The radio quiet zone, spectrum bands, communication corridors.

## Governance overlays

The governance overlays give the map its coordination function.

Assessment status. Confidence in the existing record. Unassessed, under assessment, assessed open, assessed protected. Areas under assessment carry a provisional protection notice.

Operational activity. Planned and active lunar missions with surface operating radii. Where footprints overlap, the map renders the intersection and opens an Article IX coordination note. The overlap is a fact to be examined, not a fault to be flagged.

Dynamic harms. Computed model footprints for plume surface interaction, volatile contamination, and transient electromagnetic interference. Off by default.

Disputes and salience. Challenged designations carry a small mark. The dispute register shows the challenger, the justification, and the response deadline. Visual weight by stroke and line style reflects whether a designation has survived challenge.

## How to use it

Click any point on the Moon. The popup shows four things: the feature name, its category, its tier, and what that tier means.

The Report button freezes the map and opens the full record, including source, evidence, status, assessment date, designation authority, state responsibility under Article VI, and applicable legal instruments.

## The legal foundation

The map does not create new law. It operationalises what already exists.

Article VI of the Outer Space Treaty makes states responsible for authorising and continually supervising their national operators. Every asset on the map carries the state that bears that responsibility.

Article IX requires states to conduct all their activities with due regard to the corresponding interests of all other states parties, and to consult before activities that would cause potentially harmful interference. A state cannot have reason to believe it will interfere with something it cannot see. The map makes the reason to believe visible.

Article XI asks states to share information, to the greatest extent feasible and practicable. The map does not depend on that disclosure. It is a public baseline. No registration, no invitation, no approval. Anyone can look.

## What it is not

It is not a court, a regulator, a treaty, a registry of claims, or a decision engine. It does not adjudicate, license, prohibit, or tell you where to land. It is a shared reference frame.

The map is not neutral. It makes choices about what to render visible. Its defense is process neutrality: everything is sourced, contested, and visible.

## Data sources

Each feature carries a source field. The far side radio quiet zone is protected by ITU Radio Regulations Article 22.22 and confirmed by COPUOS conference room paper A/AC.105/2024/CRP.14 on the protection of astronomy and science on the Moon. Sites of exceptional scientific importance follow the identification work of the IAU Working Group on Astronomy on the Moon.

Every feature also carries evidence grade, review status, assessment date, designation authority, and state responsibility. The data file is public and machine readable.
