# Lunar Constraint Map

An open, interactive map of the Moon that overlays five categories of resource and constraint on a single spatial reference frame, and attaches the legal obligations of the Outer Space Treaty to what it shows.

The Moon is not crowded yet, but it will be. The most contested assets are not only water ice and metals. They are landing sites, stable orbits, radio-quiet zones, sites of scientific interest, heritage, view, spectrum. These are resources in every meaningful sense, and they are already scarce.

## Layers

Extractable. Water ice in permanently shadowed regions, metals, volatiles, minerals.

Spatial. Landing sites, launch sites, stable orbits, Lagrange points.

Environmental. Permanently shadowed regions, sites of scientific interest, fragile geological features.

Intangible. Heritage sites, cultural values, the view of Earth.

Electromagnetic. The radio quiet zone, spectrum bands, communication corridors.

## The legal foundation

The map does not create new law. It operationalises what already exists.

Article VI of the Outer Space Treaty makes states responsible for authorising and continually supervising their national operators. Every asset on the map carries the state that bears that responsibility.

Article IX requires states to conduct all their activities with due regard to the corresponding interests of all other states parties, and to consult before activities that would cause potentially harmful interference. A state cannot have reason to believe it will interfere with something it cannot see. The map makes the reason to believe visible.

Article XI asks states to share information, to the greatest extent feasible and practicable. The map does not depend on that disclosure. It is a public baseline. No registration, no invitation, no approval. Anyone can look.

## Data sources

Each feature carries a source field. The far side radio quiet zone is protected by ITU Radio Regulations Article 22.22 and confirmed by COPUOS conference room paper A/AC.105/2024/CRP.14 on the protection of astronomy and science on the Moon. Sites of exceptional scientific importance follow the identification work of the IAU Working Group on Astronomy on the Moon.

## The paper

The framework is presented in the paper "Making Due Regard Visible: A Constraint Mapping Framework for Lunar Governance", accepted for the 25th Australian Space Research Conference, 29 September to 1 October 2026, Adelaide. Full text to follow after the conference.
